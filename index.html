$ man ellipse-in-plane

Draw mode (the studio way): sweep the whole ellipse onto the highlighted face in one closed stroke. Your stroke is fitted to an ellipse, then the handles appear so you can nudge it — or press R and draw it again. Handles mode skips the stroke: the centre moves the plate, the two rim dots set length + tilt, the small accent dot sets width.

Scored against the exact circle that face holds — its image is solved in closed form from the camera, not sketched in. Placement, axis angle and both radii each count; nail all three and it reads 100. Three faces per round, each more foreshortened, and after each lock the hint names whichever of the three you missed by most.

Keys: arrows move · [ ] tilt · - = length · , . width · R start the plate over · Enter lock in, then next face, then new round. Hold shift for bigger steps.

The dashed line across the sheet is the horizon — your eye level. The dashed line through the reveal is the axle: the face's normal, projected for real. An ellipse's degree is what templates are sold in — 30° means the minor axis is sin 30° = half the major.

Studio tip: the minor axis runs down the axle — on a wheel, straight out along the hub. Nail that direction before you touch the size.
